About these numbers
Source
CDC's National Notifiable Diseases Surveillance System (NNDSS) weekly table on data.cdc.gov (dataset x9gk-5huc), labels "Measles, Indigenous" and "Measles, Imported". Counts are provisional and shown as CDC published them each week.
Locally acquired
NNDSS calls these "indigenous" cases: people infected in the United States. Imported cases were infected abroad.
Added each week
The rise in the year's running total since the week before. Most measles cases reach CDC after the week they belong to, so CDC's own current-week column misses many of them. The weekly additions include those late reports, and they add up to the year's total.
4-week trend
Cases added in the latest 4 weeks compared with the 4 weeks before. A change of more than 20% either way counts as increasing or decreasing; anything smaller is stable.
% change
Not shown when last year's count at the same week was under 20, because a small base makes the percentage misleading. The tile shows both counts instead.
New York
NNDSS reports New York City separately, so "New York State (excl. NYC)" covers the rest of the state.
MMWR weeks
CDC's epidemiological weeks run Sunday through Saturday. Week 1 is the first week with at least four days in January.
