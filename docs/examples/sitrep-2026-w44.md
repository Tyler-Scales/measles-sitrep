# Measles Situation Report — MMWR Week 44, 2026 (week ending November 7, 2026)

## Summary
In MMWR week 44, 196 indigenous (U.S.-acquired) measles cases were reported nationally. The indigenous year-to-date (YTD) total is 4,991, up 219.7% from 1,561 at the same point last year. The national 4-week trend is stable (614 cases in weeks 41–44 vs. 637 in weeks 37–40), but cases are shifting between places: Wisconsin (58 cases this week) and New York State (excl. NYC) (32) are increasing, while Pennsylvania (56) is decreasing. Pennsylvania still has 29.5% of all indigenous cases this year, the largest share of any jurisdiction.

## Trend
- **National indigenous trend: stable.** There were 614 cases in weeks 41–44 and 637 in weeks 37–40, a change of −3.6%. The tool calls a trend increasing or decreasing only when the 4-week total changes by more than 20%.
- The stable national total hides changes in different places. The trend is **increasing** in Wisconsin, New York State (excl. NYC), Minnesota, and Indiana, and in the East North Central, West North Central, Mountain, and New England divisions. It is **decreasing** in Pennsylvania and the Middle Atlantic division.
- This week's 196 cases are below the largest weekly national count in the prior 52 weeks (289).

## Data notes and limitations
- **Provisional data.** NNDSS weekly counts are provisional and will be revised. Data were fetched November 13, 2026, from CDC NNDSS Weekly Data (data.cdc.gov/d/x9gk-5huc).
- **Late reports.** The national indigenous YTD rose by 363 since last week, while week 44 itself had 196 cases. The YTD therefore includes late reports and revisions for earlier weeks. The same applies to Wisconsin, Minnesota, Indiana, New York State (excl. NYC), and Pennsylvania. Weekly counts, especially recent ones, may rise as more reports arrive.
- **Possible inconsistency.** In Wisconsin, Minnesota, and Indiana, the YTD increase this week (130, 57, and 46) is larger than each jurisdiction's total for weeks 41–44 (92, 21, and 11). The available data don't show which weeks the added cases belong to. Check with those jurisdictions before reading their 4-week trends as final.
- **% change not shown.** Where last year's YTD was under 20 (for example Pennsylvania, New York State (excl. NYC), Minnesota, and Indiana), both counts are given instead of a % change.
- **Reporting areas.** "New York" in NNDSS means New York State excluding New York City. NYC is a separate reporting area.
- **Not in this source.** NNDSS weekly data do not include hospitalizations, deaths, vaccination status, ages, or links between cases and outbreaks.
- **Rankings are partial.** The tables list only the top jurisdictions. Jurisdictions not listed may still have cases.

## Recommended public health actions
1. **Concentrate outbreak response where cases are increasing.** Support case investigation, contact tracing, and post-exposure prophylaxis in Wisconsin, New York State (excl. NYC), Minnesota, and Indiana, and keep up response in Pennsylvania, which still has the largest burden.
2. **Speed up MMR catch-up vaccination.** Focus on under-vaccinated groups in affected and neighboring jurisdictions, especially in the East North Central, West North Central, and Middle Atlantic divisions.
3. **Alert clinicians.** Ask clinicians to consider measles in patients with fever and rash, isolate suspected cases right away, test promptly, and report immediately. This matters especially in jurisdictions with recent increases and in DC after its first case of the year.
4. **Reduce reporting delays.** Large late additions this week show that recent counts are incomplete. Jurisdictions should submit cases to NNDSS as quickly as they can.
5. **Keep up import prevention.** Imported cases continue to be reported (2 this week), so continue pre-travel MMR advice for international travelers and prompt exposure notification for imported cases.
